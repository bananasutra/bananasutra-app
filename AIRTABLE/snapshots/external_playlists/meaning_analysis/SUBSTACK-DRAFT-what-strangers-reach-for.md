# What Strangers Reach For (In A World Gone Bananas)

*A field report from the quietest corner of the internet, where 1,100 strangers told me what they long for — without saying a word.*

---

There's a thing SoundCloud never tells you: when a stranger takes one of your songs and files it into their own playlist, no notification goes out. No ping, no confetti, no dopamine. It just… happens, silently, in someone else's garden. Which makes it, I think, the most honest signal left on the musical internet. Nobody playlist-adds to be seen. There's no clout in it. It's the difference between applause and humming a song to yourself three days later.

So I went looking. I scraped every external playlist I could find containing my music and came back with 3,449 of these quiet little acts — my songs sitting in other people's playlists, placed there by about 1,100 strangers I've never met.

And then I asked the data a question that had nothing to do with marketing:

**In a world gone bananas, what do people actually reach for?**

## First, the confession

I should introduce myself properly, since this is the part where you decide whether to trust me. I'm Banana. I run [bananasutra.com](https://bananasutra.com), where I write quirky philosophical lyrics — a few hundred songs sorted into sutras and songbooks, asking the questions philosophy asked me first (I was corrupted young, in Paris, by the usual suspects: Camus, Russell, a well-placed "why?"). The music is made with AI tools. The words, the questions, the doubts — those are handmade.

I know, I know. "AI music is soulless garbage." I've heard it, and honestly, a lot of it is — the same way a lot of everything is. But here's the thing this dataset let me test: if the songs were empty calories, the strangers filing them into playlists at 2am would be selecting at random. Noise picks noise evenly.

They did not pick evenly. Not even close.

## What they reached for

I compared what people added against what I actually offer. My library is a big, weird, deliberately diverse buffet — satire next to lullabies, protest next to prayer, 200 songs across every mood I could cook. So if listeners just grabbed whatever was nearest, every theme would get picked in proportion to how much of it exists. That proportion is the baseline. The deviations are the message.

Here's the message.

**They reached for truth — at 1.5 times the rate I offer it.** The single most-curated song in the entire catalog, by a comical margin, is called "Tell The Truth." Not the catchiest one. Not the one I'd have bet on. The songbook that over-performed more than any other is called *Ask: (Naked) TRUTH* — picked at double its share of the shelf.

**They reached for ego-deflation — at double the baseline.** "Ego Ain't Your Amigo" and "The Great Naked King" sit at #2 and #3. Songs about the ego — seeing it, deflating it, laughing at it wearing no clothes — over-index harder than any other topic in the data. Apparently the emperor's tailor is doing great business these days, and people are hungry for someone to say what the kid in the crowd said.

**They reached for the fool — but not for foolery.** This one stopped me cold. Songs with the intention *be the fool* over-index at 2x. Songs whose *topic* is foolery — pure clowning, satire for satire's sake — under-index at half. Same jester, different job. People don't want the clown who distracts them; they want the fool who's the only one in court allowed to tell the truth. The fool as philosopher, pas le guignol.

**They reached for mortality.** My little sutra about death — bowing to it, befriending it, asking where pigeons go to die — over-indexes at 1.3. In an attention economy built on pretending we'll live forever, strangers are quietly playlisting memento mori.

**And here's what they did NOT reach for:** politics. My political satire under-performs at 0.6, and — this is the part that keeps me thinking — songs tagged with the intention *be woke* sink to 0.6 too, while *be curious*, *be wise*, and *see your ego* all rise. I don't read that as apathy. I read it as exhaustion with instruction. People are not reaching for songs that tell them what to think. They're reaching for songs that ask them what they think. Every over-performing thread in this data is a question; every under-performing one is an answer delivered with a pointed finger.

One more, my favorite: **they took the shadow with the light.** My catalog is 56% light, 44% shadow, and the playlist adds came back… 57/43. No toxic-positivity filter. No doom bias. Strangers accepted the dark exactly at the rate it was offered, as long as it came holding the light's hand. Which is, when you think about it, the entire deal we're offered by being alive.

(How they like it served, for the curious: slow, dubby, bluesy, lo-fi — moods my tagging system calls KINDLY and CHEEKY, which over-index together. Warmth with a wink. The tempo of a walk, not a chase.)

## The humble part

Now let me deflate my own ego before someone does it for me, since apparently that's on brand.

This is one artist's library on one platform. Around 1,100 strangers is a village, not humanity. Exposure and preference feed each other — songs that get found get added, songs that get added get found — and no spreadsheet fully untangles that loop. And the themes were labeled by the same suspicious character who wrote the songs. This is a lantern, not a telescope. I'm showing you what it lit up, not the whole sky.

But a lantern in a dark room is not nothing. And what it lit up was this: given a genuinely diverse buffet, with zero algorithm pushing them anywhere, people over-selected *truth, ego-work, mortality, and better questions* — and under-selected *preaching, punditry, and empty clowning*.

In a world gone bananas, that's what the quiet signals say people long for. Not more noise about the madness. A way to stand in it honestly, lightly, together.

## Why I'll keep doing this

Because the critics are half right. AI can absolutely be Artificial Indifference, Assisted Incompetence, Authoritarian Indoctrination — pick your poison, the alliteration department is fully stocked. But it can also be Alternative Intelligence. Augmented Imagination. A way for one stubborn French-American with a philosophy habit and no record deal to write four hundred songs asking whether ego is your amigo, and then discover — via 3,449 silent acts of curation — that strangers out there are asking the same questions.

The tool doesn't decide what it's for. We do. That's the whole experiment at bananasutra.com: use the new machines to smuggle the oldest questions back into the feed, three minutes at a time, wrapped in dub and banjo where the sermon can't survive but the question can.

Frank Zappa said music is the only religion that delivers the goods. Based on the data, I can now report what the congregation ordered: truth, served naked, with a side of humble pie — and yes, they kept the shadow in the bag.

Delivery's on the way.

— Banana
