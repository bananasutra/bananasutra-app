# What Strangers Reach For (In A World Gone Bananas)

*Introducing bananasutra.com: an audio testament, a few hundred little atheist prayers, and what 1,100 strangers quietly did with them.*

---

There's a thing SoundCloud never tells you: when a stranger takes one of your songs and files it into their own playlist, no notification goes out. No ping, no confetti, no dopamine hit for anyone. It just... happens, silently, in someone else's garden. Which makes it, I think, the most honest signal left on the musical internet. Nobody playlist-adds for clout. It's the difference between applause and humming a song to yourself three days later.

So I went looking. I scraped every external playlist I could find containing my music and came back with 3,449 of these quiet little acts: my songs sitting in other people's playlists, placed there by about 1,100 strangers I've never met.

Before I tell you what they reached for, I should tell you what they were reaching into.

## The confession

I'm Banana. I was corrupted young, in Paris, by the usual suspects (Camus, Russell, a well-placed *pourquoi?*) and never recovered. For the past two years I've been building [bananasutra.com](https://bananasutra.com): a few hundred songs of quirky philosophical lyrics, organized around seven sutras, seven lenses for looking at life. Know, grow, flow, glow, show, blow, bow. Plus an eighth, quackier one, for when the news demands a duck.

It's not a book. It's not a podcast, not a movie, not a gospel. It's the thing I couldn't not make: a meditation, an intention, a prayer set to basslines. An audio testament to what it feels like to be alive in a world gone bananas. Some people journal through times like these. Some march, some pray, some doomscroll. I made songs. Same thing, different noise. Each one's a little atheist prayer.

And yes: the music is made with AI tools. The words, the questions, the doubts? Handmade, every one.

I can feel some of you cringing. Stay with me. The cringe is exactly what this piece is about.

## The test

"AI music is soulless garbage." I've heard it from people I love, and a lot of AI music honestly is, the way a lot of everything is. But the accusation contains a testable claim: if the songs are empty calories, then the strangers filing them into playlists at 2am should be picking at random. Noise selects noise evenly.

They did not pick evenly.

My library is a deliberately diverse buffet. Satire next to lullabies, protest next to prayer, light next to shadow. So if listeners grabbed whatever was nearest, every theme would get picked in proportion to how much of it sits on the shelf. Against that baseline, here's what 1,100 strangers over-selected.

**Truth, at one and a half times the rate I offer it.** The most-curated song in the whole catalog, by a comical margin, is called "Tell The Truth": *"Speak the truth, no matter cost / In that truth, you'll never be lost."*

**Ego-deflation, at double the baseline.** "Ego Ain't Your Amigo" and "The Great Naked King" sit at #2 and #3. Songs about the emperor *"wearing nothing but bling-bling, oh, if only I was joking."* And here's the twist that stopped me cold: songs meant to *be the fool* over-index at 2x, while pure foolery under-indexes at half. People skip the clown who distracts them and reach for the fool who says the king is naked.

**Questions over answers.** Songs tagged *be curious* and *be wise* rise; my political satire and anything preachy sink to 0.6. What over-performs literally asks ("So, What Is Racism?" is built wall-to-wall of questions). What under-performs asserts. People aren't reaching for songs that tell them what to think. They're reaching for songs that ask them what they think.

**And the shadow with the light.** My catalog is 56% light, 44% shadow; the adds came back 57/43. No toxic-positivity filter, no doom bias. Strangers took the dark exactly at the rate it was offered, as long as it came holding the light's hand.

Honest footnote, because truth is apparently my brand: one artist, one platform, a village of strangers, themes labeled by the same suspicious character who wrote the songs. A lantern, not a telescope. But a lantern in a dark room is not nothing.

## The point

So no, I'm not here to tell you my songs are good. I'm here to tell you what the cringe is hiding.

Everything above (a few hundred songs, a thousand-plus tracks, cover art and all, written, produced, and published by one stubborn French-American with no label, no studio, and no ability to play the cello that shows up in half the mixes) happened in under two years. Spring 2024 to now. That's not me being prolific. That's the wall coming down.

This is what I wish everyone who recoils at "AI music" could see: the acronym is a choice. AI can absolutely mean Artificial Indifference, Assisted Incompetence, Authoritarian Indoctrination. The alliteration department stocks every poison, and the slop-merchants are proving it daily. But the same two letters can spell **Alternative Intelligence. Augmented Imagination. Accelerated Ideation. Arcane Innovation. Altruistic Inspiration**, even, on the good days. The tool doesn't decide which. We do.

Word processors didn't kill writing. Cameras didn't kill painting. They moved the human part (the choosing, the meaning, the *why*) to the center, and that part cannot be automated, only abandoned. When a stranger in some other timezone files "We're Tiny Specks, Right?" into their 3am playlist because it argues its way from cosmic indifference to *"so we can care... right?"*, that's not a machine connecting with a human. That's two humans connecting through a question one of them set to music, using every tool she could reach. Songwriting has always been how we share ideas we can *feel*: values we can hum before we can defend them. The new tools didn't cheapen that. They handed it to anyone who wants it.

That's the prayer bananasutra is: more Alternative Intelligence in a world drifting toward Authoritarian Indoctrination. More questions in the feed, fewer verdicts. It's my testament, and the data above is just the first proof I've found that strangers can hear it.

Frank Zappa said music is the only religion that delivers the goods. Based on the data, I can now report what the congregation quietly ordered: truth, served naked, with a side of humble pie. And the shadow kept in the bag, right where they asked for it.

The buffet is open at [bananasutra.com](https://bananasutra.com). Seven lenses, several hundred little atheist prayers, at least one song about where pigeons go to die.

So... I'm curious. What keeps you up at night? What lights your heart? What song will you write next?

— Banana
